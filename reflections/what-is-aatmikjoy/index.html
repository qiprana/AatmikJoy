GLIMPSES OF JOY · PERSONAL REFLECTION

What is AatmikJoy?

How might we recognize it? Does it have a texture, a color, a sound? While I continue to explore these questions, a few people and moments come to mind with deep gratitude.

My Grandmom

My grandmom 96 till date can read, write, tell jokes, sings classical music, can remember her primary level poems, can chant innumerable slokas and the list goes on. Where is all the energy coming from in spite of bodily illness and challenges of her life?

Every time we visit her, she cheers every single person, tells jokes and makes everyone alive again and blessing pouring on us! This is what I can describe as Aatmik Joy, which flows in abundance without being asked, asking nothing in return but just Joy of the self. This joy guides one to do what they are meant to do, and BE.

My Ayurveda Teachers

Whether it is an 8 p.m. discussion of a clinical case, 6 a.m. shloka chanting, five consecutive days of internships from 8 a.m. to 6 p.m., or a 10 p.m. call from India to teach the Ashtanga Hridayam — my Ayurveda teachers seem ever ready to teach, share and inspire.

Teaching begins with surrender and blessings sought from their lineage, gurus, devatas and Lord Dhanvantari. It ends with gratitude for the opportunity to share what has been entrusted to them.

That reverence for a lineage of knowledge, and the joy with which it is passed forward, feels deeply like AatmikJoy to me. The joy belongs not to the act of being a teacher, but to a connection within that makes sharing itself an offering.

An acquaintance

Near the end of a long week of internships, an 85-year-old gentleman came in and generously offered himself as a case for us to learn from, in the presence of a Vaidya.

After our assessment, we novice interns offered him a long list of things to do and avoid. He listened with a smile and received it all with grace. Then one of my classmates asked, out of curiosity, “What affirmations do you do?”

He paused and began reciting his morning affirmations. For the next five minutes, a class of about twenty sat speechless, simply listening.

Our assessments and suggestions suddenly felt very small beside the wisdom, inner connection and devotion he expressed so naturally. The fatigue of the long day seemed to disappear. It was as though joy had quietly filled the room, without boundaries or any need to announce itself.

“The more we begin to seek the joy within, the more we begin to see it all around us.”

These are just a few glimpses to share, and reflect on. Wishing that we all find our Joy!
